A cap, a polo and a pair of sunglasses ask different things of a wardrobe. The cap needs a place in your everyday routine. The polo needs the right fit. The sunglasses need to work on your face before the team connection matters.

That is the order to use when shopping F1 fashion collaborations. This guide looks at adidas with Mercedes-AMG PETRONAS and Audi, and Ray-Ban for Scuderia Ferrari. It combines official Singapore listings with editorial buying criteria; Arahkaii has not worn or independently tested the products. The objects in the illustrations are generic, not replicas of the items below.

## Start with the piece you would wear again

If you already wear caps, a team version is an easy item to compare with what you own. If you prefer collared tops, look at the polos before the replica driver shirts. If your current sunglasses fit badly, a collaboration is not a reason to accept the same problem in another frame.

Choose one job for the purchase. Is it an everyday item, a souvenir or something you want to collect? A souvenir can be worthwhile on its own terms. It does not need an invented claim about investment value or future scarcity to justify it.

For styling rather than shopping, the [five-outfit merch guide](/style/how-to-style-f1-merch/) shows how one team piece can work with clothes already in your wardrobe.

## Mercedes x adidas: cap or collared shirt?

For an existing wardrobe of trousers and shirts, compare the cap and engineers polo first. A cap lets you keep your preferred fabric and sleeve length in the rest of the outfit. A polo makes the team identity more central, so examine how much print you want to wear away from the circuit.

Try a polo with your own waistband height. A long hem can look different with low-rise jeans and higher-waisted tailoring. Check both the straight and seated fit; a collar that sits neatly on a hanger is only one part of the garment.

If a collection has several versions of a driver shirt, compare the exact product names and specifications. “Authentic” and “replica” are retail labels within official merchandise ranges; a product described as replica by the official seller is not, for that reason alone, counterfeit. The higher-priced version also does not automatically become the better choice for a spectator's wardrobe.

**Editorial choice:** begin with the cap if you want one clear colour note. Choose the polo if you already reach for collared tops regularly. This is a use-case recommendation, not a fabric-performance verdict.

## Audi x adidas: compare fanwear with teamwear

The [official Audi collection page](https://www.adidas.com.sg/audi_revolut_f1_team) is the starting point for distinguishing the products, rather than treating every top as the same shirt with a different price. For your own purchase, compare the silhouette, composition, print placement and wash instructions.

A simpler polo may make sense if you want something to wear with jeans or tailoring. A driver jersey may be the more satisfying souvenir if the team connection is the whole point. Neither choice needs to be defended as more authentic fandom.

Shoes require a separate decision. Do not let the convenience of a matching collection override fit. Try the pair with the socks you expect to wear and check the return conditions before walking outside in them. The [Singapore Grand Prix footwear guide](/style/comfortable-shoes-singapore-grand-prix/) explains why an existing comfortable pair may be the better race-day option even when you buy new merchandise.

**Editorial choice:** compare a familiar shirt shape before buying a full matching set. Keep a team shoe on the shortlist only if it passes the same fitting process as any other shoe.

## Ray-Ban for Ferrari: fit before the edition

Eyewear is a useful way to make the team reference smaller, but it is less forgiving of an approximate fit. Check the bridge, temple pressure and whether the frame shifts when you look down. Wear the cap you intend to pair with it so you can see whether the two compete for space.

Read the specifications for the exact model. Do not infer lens properties from a dark colour, a mirrored finish or the Ferrari name. Ask the seller about the stated UV protection, lens category and any limitations relevant to how you intend to use it. Prescription requirements need their own conversation with an optical professional.

A race-specific edition can have souvenir appeal, but that is different from everyday usefulness. Put the glasses next to the bag, shirts and cap you already own. If they only work with one specially assembled outfit, decide whether that is what you actually want from the purchase.

**Editorial choice:** use an authorised fitting and compare an ordinary model alongside the special edition. Buy the frame that suits the face, rather than a story about what it might be worth later.

## A five-question check before checkout

1. **Is this the exact product I wanted?** Compare model name, colour, size and season, not just the leading image.
2. **Who is selling it?** Start from the brand's official links or an authorised retailer. A familiar logo in a marketplace listing does not identify the seller.
3. **What is the full cost?** Check currency, delivery, taxes and any membership or promotion conditions.
4. **Can I return it?** Read the policy for this seller and this product, including exclusions for customisation or final-sale stock.
5. **When will I use it next?** Name a real outfit or occasion. A souvenir answer is acceptable; an imagined resale windfall is not necessary.

If an item is needed for an event the same day, an online delivery estimate is not equivalent to confirmed local collection. Ask the store about stock and collection before travelling or placing an order on that assumption.

## Keep one purchase, not five compromises

The most useful shortlist may contain only a cap and a polo, with no shoes or sunglasses at all. Put each beside clothes you already wear, assess its fit, then leave the rest for another day.
