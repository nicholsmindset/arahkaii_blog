**Start with the kind of cinema you want to encounter, then check the rating, running time and practicalities. The programme is a set of invitations, not a list to complete.**

The 37th Singapore International Film Festival's published dates are **21 October to 1 November 2026**. If you are choosing a visit for the final weekend, begin with the actual screening, not just the film's place in the catalogue. Some programmes have already had their only listed session; others still have dates within the festival's final days.

This guide compares the organiser's published listings, ratings and ticketing terms, checked on **25 October**, for a planned 28 October publication. It is a practical shortlist, not a review or a live seat checker. We have not watched these films, attended the screenings or verified remaining ticket inventory. Confirm the exact session and availability through the [official festival schedule](https://sgiff.eventive.org/schedule) before making plans.

## Choose a question, then a section

A first festival visit does not need to begin with the most prominent guest or the most difficult-to-book screening. Start with the reason you want to spend an evening in the cinema. Would you like to encounter local filmmakers, try shorter work or see how an artist stretches the screen beyond a conventional feature?

The organiser's [section guide](https://sgiff.com/festival-programmes/) gives useful entry points. Our comparison turns those curatorial categories into decisions a newcomer can make.

| Your starting interest | Section to investigate | What to check before booking |
|---|---|---|
| Local filmmakers | Singapore Panorama | The complete programme, not only one short's title |
| Short-form regional work | Southeast Asian Short Film Competition | Total session duration and the combined programme's rating |
| Asian feature filmmaking | Asian Feature Film Competition | Running time, language, subtitles and subject matter |
| Work across performance and moving image | Undercurrent | Whether the event is a screening, performance or another format |
| Restored cinema | Landmark | The specific restoration, language and presentation |
| A first visit with a child | Junior Explorers | Individual film rating and whether that session is designated relaxed |

These are routes into the programme, not rankings. “Competition” describes a festival section; it is not a guarantee that its film will be your favourite. Similarly, a short-film programme can cover several moods or difficult subjects. A shorter individual film does not automatically mean an easier session.

For a first visit, choose one clear preference and one modest stretch. A viewer who usually watches narrative features might try a short-film programme. Someone already interested in visual art might investigate the format of an Undercurrent event. The stretch should be in the work, not in a frantic journey between venues.

## Five listed screenings to compare for the final weekend

These organiser-listed sessions fall on 31 October or 1 November. They offer different lengths, age classifications and viewing interests; inclusion here is not an artistic endorsement or a claim that tickets remain. All times are Singapore local time. The links lead to each film's official listing.

| Film | Listed session and venue | Length and classification | Planning distinction |
|---|---|---|---|
| [The Invisibles](https://sgiff.com/film/the-invisibles/) | 31 October, 4.30pm; Golden Village Bugis+, Hall 5 | 110 minutes; PG13, some frightening scenes | A longer Junior Explorers option; read the advisory as well as the family-section label. |
| [Julián](https://sgiff.com/film/julian/) | 1 November, 10.30am; Golden Village Bugis+, Hall 5 | 85 minutes; PG | The shortest film in this shortlist; the November listing is not marked as a relaxed screening. |
| [Hanabi](https://sgiff.com/film/fire-flower/) | 1 November, 11am; Oldham Theatre | 107 minutes; NC16, some coarse language | An Undercurrent feature, distinct from that section's live performances. |
| [Brave Cat](https://sgiff.com/film/brave-cat/) | 1 November, 4.30pm; Golden Village Bugis+, Hall 7 | 92 minutes; PG | An afternoon Junior Explorers choice; check Hall 7 rather than assuming the same room as the morning film. |
| [Amores Perros](https://sgiff.com/film/amores-perros/) | 1 November, 7.30pm; Oldham Theatre | 154 minutes; M18, intimate scenes | A Landmark selection for an adult evening; the running time alone reaches roughly 10.04pm before any additional programme time. |

The contrast is useful even before reading a synopsis. *Julián* and *Hanabi* overlap, so they are alternatives rather than a morning double bill. The 154-minute *Amores Perros* asks for a different evening commitment from an 85-minute film. Its approximate finish above is our addition of the published running time to the start, not an organiser-confirmed exit time.

At Oldham Theatre, the [Asian Film Archive's venue page](https://asianfilmarchive.org/whatson/about-oldham-theatre/) places the cinema at the National Archives of Singapore, **1 Canning Rise**. Use the festival's dated screening terms for your event; a venue's general weekly schedule is not a substitute for the festival booking. Save the venue and hall alongside the ticket, particularly when comparing sessions in different buildings.

## For families, distinguish the film from the screening format

The [Junior Explorers section](https://sgiff.com/festival-programmes/junior-explorers/) is an entry point, not a single age recommendation. A PG title at 85 minutes and a PG13 title at 110 minutes make different demands. Read the synopsis and advisory, consider your child's tolerance for length and subject matter, and give them a say where appropriate.

There is also a concrete timing trap. The official *Brave Cat* and *Julián* pages identify their **25 October** sessions at the National Museum as relaxed screenings. Their **1 November** listings are at Golden Village Bugis+ and are not labelled relaxed. Do not carry the earlier session's sensory-friendly format into the final-weekend booking.

The distinction affects price as well as experience. SGIFF's [ticketing terms](https://sgiff.com/ticketing/) restrict the S$20 Junior Explorers adult-and-child combo to relaxed screenings; it admits one adult and one child below 16. That is not evidence that the 1 November sessions qualify. Check the actual session and checkout instead of budgeting from a section-wide promotion.

A shorter film need not mean a shorter ticketed programme. The [Undercurrent page](https://sgiff.com/festival-programmes/undercurrent/) lists an **84-minute, R21 triple bill on 1 November at 1.30pm, Oldham Theatre**. It contains the 17-minute *In Sum*, whose individual listing is PG13. Looking at that short's length and rating alone would misrepresent the session. Read the combined programme classification and all its contents before deciding.

## Book the event you mean to attend

[SGIFF's ticketing page](https://sgiff.com/ticketing/) lists a general film ticket at S$15; special presentations and Undercurrent performances are S$20, and opening or closing tickets are S$25. The final-weekend shortlist does not imply every film is in the general-price category. Use the specific event's checkout price, including any applicable conditions.

The organiser sells and manages tickets digitally and asks visitors to check ratings before purchase. Tickets are generally non-refundable and non-exchangeable, with refunds for cancelled or rescheduled films. Read the current terms before paying, particularly if you are trying to connect two sessions.

A pass is only good value if it covers the events you want and you can realistically attend them. Make a personal list of eligible sessions first, compare their individual prices, then check any reservation or access conditions. Buying an ambitious number of screenings is not the same as making time to watch them.

Bring the confirmed e-ticket and whatever identification is required. Check the venue as carefully as the start time; a festival name is not a cinema address. Leave a real interval for food, transport and finding the room. If a post-screening conversation is part of the attraction, ask whether it is included in the listed session duration before booking an onward commitment.

## A sold-out screening is not a reliable plan

SGIFF describes a Rush Queue for unfilled seats, but entry is not guaranteed. At the time of our check, different parts of the ticketing page gave different advice about when to arrive for that queue. We therefore do not repeat one arrival interval as a settled rule. Confirm with the organiser if you intend to use it.

Treat the queue as an optional attempt, not the foundation of an evening with several people or a long journey attached. Agree beforehand what you will do if nobody gets in—or if only some of the group do. That small decision is more useful than assuming persistence will produce a seat.

For confirmed bookings, arrive before the advertised start. A published grace period is an emergency boundary, not a recommended arrival time. The film, the other viewers and your own experience benefit from being settled before it begins.

## Make one screening count

After the credits, resist the urge to turn the film immediately into a score. Start with something specific: a sound, a cut, a performance or a question the ending leaves open. If a filmmaker conversation takes place, a concrete question about a choice in the work is often more productive than asking what the entire film “means”. This is viewing advice, not a report of a particular festival exchange.

The festival can be a way into cinema you will follow throughout the year. Save the filmmaker's name and look for future authorised screenings or releases. Do not assume a title will arrive on a particular streaming service without a distributor announcement.

For this festival, choose one workable session, read its full listing and verify it on the organiser's schedule before booking. A catalogue can remain online after its screening dates have passed; this guide is for the final weekend of the 2026 edition, not a continuing cinema programme.
