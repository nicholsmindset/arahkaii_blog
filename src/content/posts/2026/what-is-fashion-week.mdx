It's 8pm in Singapore on the first Tuesday of October. You're on the MRT home, thumb on autopilot, and suddenly every third post is the same thing: a model walking very fast through a very empty room in Paris, a celebrity in sunglasses at 9am local time, and a caption that just says "SS27." You've seen it every season. You've never been told what any of it actually means.

This is that explainer. No gatekeeping, no glossary you need a glossary for. Welcome to *Fashion, Translated*, our new series that takes the runway, the jargon and the industry inside-jokes and puts them into plain English. And we're starting with the big one.

## So, what is fashion week?

**Fashion week is a scheduled run of runway shows and presentations where designers reveal their next collection to press, buyers and influencers, usually around six months before it reaches stores. The "Big Four" happen back-to-back in New York, London, Milan and Paris every February–March and September–October.**

That's the whole thing, really. Everything else is detail. But the detail is where it gets interesting.

## The Big Four, explained like a group chat

Twice a year, the industry moves in a loop it calls "fashion month." The main womenswear circuit generally runs in the same order, and each city has its own personality. For the Spring/Summer 2027 womenswear season, the organisers listed the following dates. The city descriptions are editorial shorthand:

| City | Who runs it | SS27 dates (2026) | The vibe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| New York | [CFDA](https://cfda.com/news/view-the-preliminary-september-2026-official-nyfw-schedule/) | 10–15 Sept | Commercial, celebrity-heavy, wearable American sportswear |
| London | [British Fashion Council](https://www.britishfashioncouncil.co.uk/London-Fashion-Week) | 17–21 Sept | The experimental one. Young designers, art-school energy, risk-taking |
| Milan | [Camera Nazionale della Moda Italiana](https://www.cameramoda.it/en/) | 22–28 Sept | Craft, leather, family-run heritage houses, serious tailoring |
| Paris | [FHCM](https://www.fhcm.paris/en/paris-fashion-week) | 28 Sept–6 Oct | The main event. The biggest houses, the biggest budgets, the longest schedule |

Paris always goes last, and that's on purpose. It's the finale, the city where the heavyweights (Chanel, Dior, Hermès, Louis Vuitton, Saint Laurent) show. Check the [FHCM calendar](https://www.fhcm.paris/en/paris-fashion-week) for the full Paris programme.

As an editorial shorthand, rather than a rule about every designer: New York tells you what people will buy. London tells you what people will be talking about in three years. Milan tells you how it should be made. Paris tells you what the most powerful brands on earth have decided you'll want.

## Wait, why is it called "SS27" if it's 2026?

<Figure src={seasonImage} alt="A muslin toile, garment rail and fabric swatches in a design studio" caption="AI-generated conceptual illustration; not a documentary photograph or a named brand product." credit="Arahkaii / ChatGPT image model" ratio="16x9" />

This is the single most confusing thing about fashion week, so let's clear it up.

Designers show collections roughly six months *before* the season they're meant for. So the shows that ran in September and October 2026 were for **Spring/Summer 2027** (SS27). The shows in February and March 2027 will be for **Fall/Winter 2027** (FW27, sometimes written AW27 for Autumn/Winter).

That six-month gap exists for a practical reason. After the show, buyers from department stores and boutiques place their orders. Factories then produce the pieces. Then the clothes ship to stores. The runway is basically the pitch deck. The store rack is the product launch.

So when you see a sheer linen coat on a Paris runway in October, the realistic timeline is: it lands in stores early the following year, filters into high-street versions a few months later, and shows up on your Pinterest board somewhere in between.

## Plot twist: there's more than one fashion week

"Fashion week" usually means the womenswear shows above. But the industry calendar is busier than that. Here's the full year in one glance, using the 2026 calendar:

| When | What | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| January | Menswear FW + Haute Couture SS | Men's shows in Milan and Paris; couture in Paris (SS26 couture ran 26–29 Jan) |
| Feb–March | Womenswear FW (the Big Four) | The autumn/winter edition of fashion month |
| May–June | Resort / Cruise | Destination shows, often in a far-flung location, between the main seasons |
| June | Menswear SS | Paris men's SS27 ran 23–28 June |
| July | Haute Couture FW | Paris couture FW26 ran 6–9 July |
| Sept–Oct | Womenswear SS (the Big Four) | The spring/summer edition, the one that just ended |
| Dec | Pre-Fall | Quieter, commercial collections that hang in stores the longest |

*Haute couture* is its own universe: made-to-measure, handmade, eye-wateringly expensive and governed by strict rules in Paris. We break it down properly in our next explainer on couture vs ready-to-wear. For now, know that what you see in fashion month is **ready-to-wear**: clothes made in standard sizes, meant to be sold in stores.

## What actually happens at a show

<Figure src={showImage} alt="An empty runway hall with rows of benches and a blank show-notes card" caption="AI-generated conceptual illustration; not a documentary photograph or a named brand product." credit="Arahkaii / ChatGPT image model" ratio="16x9" />

If you imagine fashion week as a glamorous, hours-long gala, prepare to be underwhelmed. Most runway shows last between 10 and 15 minutes. Some are over before the latecomers have found their seats.

Here's the anatomy of a typical show:

1. **The invite.** Physical invites are a flex in themselves. Brands send sculptures, books, even edible objects. Who gets one, and where they're seated, is decided weeks ahead.
2. **The venue.** Museums, abandoned buildings, gardens, purpose-built sets. The set is part of the message, which is why people talk about the room as much as the clothes.
3. **The looks.** A collection is shown as a numbered series of "looks": complete outfits, styled head to toe. A show might have 40 to 60 of them.
4. **The finale.** All the models walk one last lap together, then the designer pops out for a bow, usually in jeans and a T-shirt, looking like they haven't slept. 
5. **The show notes.** A short text left on every seat explaining the idea behind the collection. Critics read them. Everyone else photographs them for Instagram.
6. **The review.** Within hours, critics publish their verdicts. Within a day, the internet has decided whether the collection is "a moment" or "a miss."

Then everyone races across the city to the next show. In Paris, there can be eight or nine in a single day.

## Who's in the room (and why your feed fills up)

A fashion show audience is a carefully engineered mix, and every group is there for a reason.

**Editors and critics** write the reviews that shape how a collection is remembered. **Buyers** from stores decide what actually gets produced in volume, which makes them quietly the most powerful people in the room. **Celebrities and brand ambassadors** sit front row because their photos travel further than any ad campaign. **Creators** do the same thing at a smaller, more targeted scale. And **street-style photographers** outside the venue turn the queue itself into content.

That front row (insiders call it the "FROW") isn't just a status symbol. It's media strategy. When a K-drama actor or a pop star walks into a show, the brand gets a wave of photos, clips and fan edits that can be worth millions in what the industry calls earned media value (EMV): the estimated value of all that free publicity.

So when your feed suddenly fills with one celebrity's airport outfit and arrival video, that's not a coincidence. That's the system working exactly as designed.

## How to read a season without chasing every headline

Start with the official calendar, then choose three collections to compare. Look for a repeated silhouette, a material you would actually wear and a styling idea that works with clothes you own. A runway look is a proposal, not a shopping list.

For Singapore, our useful filter is practical: would the fabric, layering and coverage work outdoors, in air conditioning and on your usual commute? Keep the proportion or colour combination even if the runway’s heavy coat makes little sense here.

## Why any of this matters to you

Fair question. You're not buying a $5,000 Paris runway coat. Most people aren't. But fashion week still shapes your wardrobe more than you'd think.

Ideas move downhill. A silhouette, colour or detail that appears across several big shows tends to get picked up by trend forecasters, then by mid-price brands, then by high-street and online retailers. By the time a trend reaches your local mall, it has usually passed through four or five filters. Knowing the source helps you distinguish an enduring idea from a short-lived trend. Our [feature on textile authenticity](/culture/batik-blockchain-indonesia-textile-authenticity-technology/) considers another useful question: how the clothes were made.

It's also how you shop smarter. If you can see where a trend came from, you can decide whether it's worth buying into, or whether the "inspired by" version is a better deal. That's the whole point of *Fashion, Translated*: knowing more so you can spend less, and better.

## Does fashion week even reach Singapore?

More than you'd expect, and in two directions.

First, the trickle-down. Luxury houses ship their collections to Singapore flagships along Orchard Road and at Marina Bay, and regional buyers are in those showrooms in Paris and Milan placing orders for this part of the world.

Second, Singapore’s own designers are pushing outwards. The regional context also extends to [Jakarta Fashion Week](/style/jakarta-fashion-week-2025/). In September 2026, the Singapore Fashion Council launched [*Singapore: Selects*](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/singaporefashioncouncil_singapore-selects-concludes-its-inaugural-activity-7504082874787155968-p674), a new platform that took 18 homegrown fashion and lifestyle brands to Seoul's Seongsu district, timed to run alongside Seoul Fashion Week. It was curated by DORS at Design Orchard and backed by Enterprise Singapore.

At home, the calendar is more community-led than corporate. [Orchard Road Fashion Week](https://www.scape.sg/whats-on/orchard-road-fashion-week/) ran at \*SCAPE in August 2026 with 42 brands across 25 shows, featuring emerging labels from Singapore, the Philippines, Indonesia, Thailand, India and beyond. Check each local organiser’s current programme for public access and ticket arrangements.

## How to "do" fashion week from Singapore

You don't need an invite. You need a plan. Here's how to follow along like an insider without staying up until 3am.

1. **Watch the livestreams on replay.** Most major houses stream their shows on their own sites and social channels. Paris shows can fall in the afternoon, evening or overnight in Singapore, depending on their scheduled time, so watch over breakfast instead.
2. **Look at the full collection, not just the viral clip.** The one look that goes viral is rarely the one that sells. Scroll the whole lineup on the brand's site or a runway database.
3. **Read one good review per show.** It'll tell you what the designer was trying to say. That context turns "nice dress" into "oh, I get it."
4. **Spot the repeats.** If you see the same idea across three or more big houses, that's a trend. If you only see it once, it's a designer's personal mood.
5. **Translate it for the tropics.** This is the step most fashion coverage skips, and the one that matters most here.

## How to wear it in Singapore

<Figure src={tropicsImage} alt="A woman wearing an ivory linen overshirt and olive trousers in tropical shade" caption="AI-generated conceptual illustration; not a documentary photograph or a named brand product." credit="Arahkaii / ChatGPT image model" ratio="16x9" />

Runway collections are designed with a European calendar in mind. Our reality is tropical heat, humidity and offices cold enough to store vegetables. So run every trend through three filters:

- **Fabric first.** A runway look in heavy wool becomes linen, cotton poplin or a lightweight crepe here. The silhouette is the trend, not the material.
- **Layer for aircon, not for weather.** Spring/summer jackets and sheer overshirts work brilliantly in Singapore. You'll wear them indoors, not out.
- **Modesty is a styling choice, not a limitation.** Longer hemlines, loose tailoring and fluid layers offer useful styling options. Pair a slip silhouette with a fine-knit long-sleeve underneath, or a wide-leg trouser with a longline shirt, and the look stays current while covering up.

## Sound like you know: 3 terms to use correctly

- **RTW (ready-to-wear):** Clothes made in standard sizes and sold in stores, as opposed to made-to-measure couture. Also called *prêt-à-porter*.
- **Look:** One complete runway outfit, numbered in the show order. "Look 12" means the twelfth outfit, styled head to toe.
- **Pre-fall / Resort:** The "in-between" collections released between the main seasons. Less hype, but they often hang in stores the longest and make the most money.

## FAQ

**What are the Big Four fashion weeks?**
New York, London, Milan and Paris. They run back-to-back twice a year, in February–March and September–October, in that order, with Paris closing the season.

**Why are September 2026 shows called SS27?**
Designers show about six months ahead. Collections shown in September and October 2026 are for Spring/Summer 2027, giving buyers time to order and factories time to produce.

**Can the public attend fashion week?**
The Big Four shows are invite-only, but most major houses livestream them. In Singapore, community events such as Orchard Road Fashion Week sell public tickets.

**How long is a fashion show?**
Most runway shows last 10 to 15 minutes, with around 40 to 60 looks.

**Does Singapore have a fashion week?**
Not a Big Four-style official week. Singapore's scene runs through events like Orchard Road Fashion Week, plus Singapore Fashion Council platforms such as Singapore: Selects in Seoul.
