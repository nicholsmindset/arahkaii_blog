Little India’s Deepavali season brings illuminated streets, festive shopping and opportunities to learn about Indian heritage. This guide pairs the organisers’ published programme with a suggested evening route; it is not a report from an on-site visit.

## The key dates at a glance

**[Deepavali falls on Sunday, 8 November 2026](https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/public-holidays), with Monday, 9 November as the public holiday.** But the celebrations start weeks before.

| Deepavali Festival Village | Nightly from 7pm, 10 Oct to 8 Nov | Campbell Lane |

| Sangeetham Under the Stars (free outdoor music) | Sat 31 Oct, 8pm to 10pm | Campbell Lane |

[LISHA’s light-up programme](https://deepavali.sg/events/street-light-up-ceremony) lists 3 October and nightly hours of 7pm to midnight. Its page does not give a closing date. Check the organiser before planning a late-November visit.

## 1. Walk the light-up (properly)

The lights stretch over two kilometres along Serangoon Road, and the whole thing is designed to be walked. Start at the southern end near Tekka Centre, where the first big archway marks the entrance, and head north. The arches change design along the way, often featuring peacocks, lotus flowers and oil lamps, the classic Deepavali symbols of beauty, purity and light winning over darkness.

**Pro tip:** don't just look up. The best photos happen at street level, framing the arches with the garland stalls, sari shops and shophouses below. And walk the side streets too. Some of the prettiest corners are off the main road.

## 2. Get lost in the Festival Village

<Figure src={bazaarImage} alt="Brass and terracotta lamps beside jasmine garlands at an imagined festive stall" caption="AI-generated conceptual illustration; not a documentary photograph or a named brand product." credit="Arahkaii / ChatGPT image model" ratio="16x9" />

The [Deepavali Festival Village](https://deepavali.sg/events/festival-village) on Campbell Lane is where the energy is. It's part bazaar, part open-air stage, and it runs nightly from 7pm until Deepavali itself on 8 November.

The [Sangeetham programme on 31 October](https://deepavali.sg/events/sangeetham-under-the-stars) mixes Carnatic classical music, fusion sets, Bollywood nights and a showcase for emerging artists. Between performances, wander the stalls along Campbell Lane and Hastings Road for:

- **Ethnic wear and jewellery:** bangles, jhumkas, embroidered juttis and last-minute dupattas
- **Home decor:** brass diyas, rangoli powders, torans (doorway hangings) and fairy lights
- **Festive snacks:** jars of murukku, mixture and sweets, many made fresh for the season
- **Henna:** quick, beautiful designs, usually done right there at the stall

Bring some cash; not every stall takes cards.

## 4. Eat your way through the sweets

<Figure src={sweetsImage} alt="Murukku, mysore pak, laddu and gulab jamun arranged on brass dishes" caption="AI-generated conceptual illustration; not a documentary photograph or a named brand product." credit="Arahkaii / ChatGPT image model" ratio="16x9" />

Deepavali is a festival you can taste. Homes prepare trays of sweets and savouries to share with visitors, and Little India's sweet shops go into overdrive. Here's your cheat sheet:

- **Murukku:** crunchy spirals made from rice flour and urad dal, pressed and deep-fried. The name roughly means "to twist" in Tamil.
- **Adhirasam:** a dark, chewy, jaggery-sweetened fried pastry, a South Indian Deepavali classic.
- **Mysore pak:** a rich, crumbly sweet made with gram flour, ghee and sugar.
- **Laddu:** round sweets made from flour, sugar and ghee, often studded with cashews.
- **Palkova:** a soft, milky fudge whose name comes from the Tamil for milk and thickened milk sweet.
- **Gulab jamun:** soft dough balls soaked in fragrant syrup. Dangerously easy to eat five of.

**Where to find them:** the stalls at the Festival Village, the sweet shops around Little India Arcade on Serangoon Road, and the hawker stalls at Tekka Centre.

**On halal status:** Vegetarian food is not automatically halal-certified. Check the current certificate for the specific outlet and ask about ingredients and preparation; this guide has not verified individual stalls.

## 5. Visit with respect: temples and mosques

Little India is a living neighbourhood, and its places of worship are at the heart of the season.

**Sri Veeramakaliamman Temple** on Serangoon Road and **Sri Srinivasa Perumal Temple** further up the same road are two of Singapore's most important Hindu temples, and both are busy with special prayers in the run-up to Deepavali. Visitors are generally welcome outside prayer times. Cover your shoulders and knees, take off your shoes at the entrance, ask before photographing people, and step aside for worshippers.

Little India is also home to historic mosques, including **Abdul Gafoor Mosque** on Dunlop Street and **Masjid Angullia** on Serangoon Road. If you're Muslim and planning an evening around the lights, they're convenient for Maghrib and Isyak before or after your walk. It's a quiet reminder of what makes this neighbourhood, and Singapore's festive season, so special: many faiths, one street, everyone celebrating together.

## 6. Dress the part

You don't have to, but it's a lot more fun when you do. Wear something bright and comfortable, and choose shoes you can walk in for two hours. Slip-on styles make temple visits easier.

## A perfect Saturday evening in Little India

Here's a route that fits it all in, no rushing.

1. **4pm:** Arrive at Little India MRT. Head to the Indian Heritage Centre on Campbell Lane if the day’s programme and opening hours suit your visit.
2. **5.30pm:** Browse the textile shops around Little India, then stop for an early dinner at Tekka Centre, checking the specific outlet’s certification if you need halal food.
3. **6.30pm:** Walk up Serangoon Road to Sri Veeramakaliamman Temple and pay a respectful visit. Muslim readers can pray Maghrib at Abdul Gafoor Mosque, a short walk away.
4. **7pm:** The lights switch on. Walk the arches north along Serangoon Road and take your photos.
5. **8pm:** Loop back to Campbell Lane for the Festival Village performances and bazaar.
6. **9pm:** Stock up on murukku and sweets for the family, then head home.

## Practical tips before you go

<Figure src={walkingkitImage} alt="Water bottle and umbrella in a cotton tote beside comfortable slip-on shoes" caption="AI-generated conceptual illustration; not a documentary photograph or a named brand product." credit="Arahkaii / ChatGPT image model" ratio="16x9" />

- **Getting there:** Little India MRT (NE7/DT12) puts you right at the start of the light-up. Farrer Park MRT (NE8) is handy for the northern end. Driving is not worth it; parking is very limited.
- **Best time to visit:** weekday evenings are far calmer. The final two weekends before Deepavali, 31 October to 1 November and 7 to 8 November, will be the busiest.
- **Check conditions:** Check the weather and current air-quality updates before a long outdoor evening.
- **Stay hydrated:** carry water, and duck into Tekka Centre or a shop to cool down between walks.
- **Mind the crowds:** keep bags in front, especially around the Festival Village on weekends.
- **It's free:** the street light-up is free; check individual programme listings for admission and workshop charges. Budget only for food, shopping and any ticketed workshops.

Deepavali celebrates light winning over darkness, and there's no better place in Singapore to feel it than Little India. Go early, go hungry, wear something bright, and stay until the street is glowing. Happy Deepavali.

## FAQ

**When is the Little India Deepavali light-up in 2026?**
The lights were switched on on 3 October 2026 and come on nightly from 7pm to midnight, with the closing date still to be confirmed.

**Is the Little India light-up free?**
The street light-up is free. Check individual event and museum listings for admission arrangements and workshop charges.

**When is the Deepavali Festival Village?**
It runs nightly from 7pm on Campbell Lane, from 10 October to 8 November 2026.

**What is the nearest MRT to the Deepavali light-up?**
Little India MRT (NE7/DT12). Farrer Park MRT (NE8) is closer to the northern end.

**Is the food in Little India halal?**
Certification varies by outlet. Check the current MUIS certificate for the specific stall or restaurant; vegetarian food is not automatically halal-certified.
