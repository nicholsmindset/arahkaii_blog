**A snowy city and a cold-weather city break ask different things of a first-time traveller. Comparing official guidance makes the trade-off clearer.**

Choose Sapporo if experiencing a snowy winter is central to the trip and you are willing to plan around it. Choose Tokyo if the main attraction is the city in cooler weather and snow would be an optional surprise. Choose Seoul for a city-and-culture trip in genuinely cold conditions, without making snowfall the measure of success. None of these choices comes with a forecast for your dates, but the distinction prevents an expensive misunderstanding.

[JNTO's Tokyo guide](https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/kanto/tokyo/) describes cold, dry winters and rare snow. [Sapporo City's winter advice](https://www.city.sapporo.jp/kensetsu/yuki/kanko.html) addresses icy pavements, heavy snowfall and difficult road conditions. The Seoul municipal [2026 visitor guide](https://english.seoul.go.kr/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/seoulguidebook_eng.pdf) describes a mostly cold, dry winter with snow on some days. These are three different practical starting points. This guide focuses on city-based holidays, not skiing or a ranking of every Asian winter destination.

## Compare the demands before comparing the scenery

| Decision | Sapporo | Tokyo | Seoul |
|---|---|---|---|
| Choose it for | Snow as a central part of a city holiday | A city break in cooler weather | Korean culture and a cold-weather city stay |
| Main preparation | Icy walking surfaces and weather disruption | Warm layers, plus a plan that does not require snow | Warm clothing and shorter outdoor stretches |
| Useful indoor anchor | Central underground walking network | Tokyo National Museum, when open | National Museum of Korea, subject to gallery closures |
| Calendar trap | A snow photograph does not establish your dates' conditions | Significant December museum restrictions | Holiday and gallery closures need checking separately |
| Poor fit | Travellers unwilling to negotiate wintry surfaces | A trip that will disappoint without snow | Assuming cold weather guarantees falling snow |

This is an editorial comparison of official visitor guidance, not a test of routes, boots or hotels. No temperature or snowfall figure here is being presented as a December 2026 forecast.

## Sapporo: choose a base you can return to easily

For a first trip, make one hotel your base and resist attaching a different excursion to every day. The city authority's winter guidance warns that surfaces vary with temperature and snowfall. A journey that looks straightforward on a summer map can require more attention in winter.

That makes the route from the hotel entrance to transport important. Before booking, examine the whole connection: outdoor sections, steps, luggage handling and the route back when somebody is tired. “Near the station” does not explain all of those things. Ask the property about the access that matters to your group rather than relying on a distance label.

Sapporo's official advice points visitors towards suitable winter clothing and walking guidance and recommends checking weather information. Treat this as practical preparation. A visually convincing winter outfit is not evidence that its shoes work on a particular icy surface. Test fit with the socks you intend to wear, and use the city's guidance when choosing equipment.

Keep the arrival day modest. A first encounter with cold, luggage and an unfamiliar route is enough of an activity. Put any outdoor excursion on a day that can move, and make sure its operator has clear weather and cancellation arrangements. Check the actual facility’s opening notice and booking conditions before paying; a city winter guide cannot establish that a particular slope or snow-play area will be operating.

## Sapporo: separate the airport journey from the last kilometre

[JR Hokkaido’s Airport Train guide](https://www.jrhokkaido.co.jp/global/english/travel/airport.html) distinguishes non-reserved travel from a reserved seat. Non-reserved seats do not need a reservation; a reserved seat requires the basic fare **and** a reserved-seat ticket. The reserved-seat e-ticket alone does not open the ticket gate. That is an easy distinction to miss when arranging the trip from a phone.

The operator advertises a fastest New Chitose–Sapporo journey of 33 minutes. Treat that as a train service figure, not an airport-to-hotel estimate or a winter punctuality guarantee. Immigration, baggage collection, the wait for a service and the final journey to your accommodation sit outside it. A pre-booked dinner immediately after a flight creates pressure the advertised train time does not solve.

For the city itself, the official [Chi-Ka-Ho visitor page](https://www.sapporo.travel/spot/facility/chikaho/) identifies a **520-metre** underground link between Sapporo and Odori subway stations, within the wider underground network running towards Susukino. This is a useful way to reduce exposure, but it is not proof that every hotel or attraction has a sheltered entrance. Check the exact exit, lifts and final outdoor section for the property you are considering.

A sensible first full day can therefore begin with the central network, a short outdoor look at Odori when conditions allow, and a return to warmth before committing to a farther trip. This is a suggested structure, not a timed or accessibility-tested route. If the outdoor section becomes uncomfortable, shorten it; do not turn the first day into an endurance test because the map makes the distances look small.

## Tokyo: let winter change the pace, not the purpose

JNTO’s description of rare snow is the reason to build this trip around the city rather than a white landscape. Check the relevant railway’s operating notices when travelling; a general climate description cannot establish whether a particular service will run.

The useful proposition is therefore straightforward: visit Tokyo because you want Tokyo. Build a day around one area, with an indoor stop you would choose in its own right. Keep warm layers easy to remove when moving inside rather than making every transition a major repacking exercise.

If snow is non-negotiable, a separate excursion needs separate research. Verify the destination's conditions, operating dates, transport, clothing needs and cancellation rules. A general statement that snow exists within reach of Tokyo does not establish that your chosen day trip will work.

Do not plan a remote snow excursion on the day of an international departure. That is our planning recommendation, not a claim about a route's punctuality. The cost of one missed connection can be much larger than the value of adding one last activity.

A museum sounds like a dependable winter anchor until its calendar contradicts the itinerary. The [Tokyo National Museum’s published visitor information](https://www.tnm.jp/modules/r_free_page/index.php?id=113&lang=en), checked on 25 October, makes late December 2026 a particularly important case:

- **15 December:** the museum lists a facility-inspection closure.
- **22–25 December:** only the Gallery of Horyuji Treasures and Kuroda Memorial Hall are listed as open.
- **26–31 December:** year-end closure.
- **1 January 2027:** a published special opening from noon to 8pm.

There are additional room closures, so confirm the galleries you actually want. Those dates are not a general rule for other museums or a promise against later changes. They show why a saved photograph of the Honkan is not enough to plan a visit.

For a date when your preferred galleries are open, make Ueno the day's main area rather than attaching the museum to a cross-city checklist. The museum lists a ten-minute walk from JR Ueno or Uguisudani station; allow your own pace and weather margin. Its [current visitor guidelines](https://www.tnm.jp/modules/r_free_page/index.php?id=127) also prohibit suitcases. Leave luggage with an appropriate storage provider or accommodation instead of treating an indoor attraction as a stop on the way to the airport.

The planning lesson extends beyond Tokyo: an indoor backup should have a name, an opening date and a way of getting there. “We’ll find a museum” leaves the most useful decision until somebody is already cold.

Seoul’s [municipal climate guide](https://english.seoul.go.kr/seoul-views/meaning-of-seoul/3-climate/) describes a very cold winter influenced by strong winds and cold northerly air. Choose it because you want the city’s cultural life, rather than as a compromise that will necessarily be gentle or snowy. An attractive outdoor photograph does not tell you how long your group will be comfortable standing still.

The [National Museum of Korea](https://www.museum.go.kr/ENG/main/index.do) offers a substantial indoor anchor at 137 Seobinggo-ro, Yongsan-gu. Its published ordinary hours are 9.30am–5.30pm on Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, Friday and Sunday, and until 9pm on Wednesday and Saturday; admission closes 30 minutes before the end. General admission is free, while special exhibitions may charge.

Do not assume the whole collection is available. The museum’s [permanent-exhibition information](https://vcm.museum.go.kr/ENG/contents/E0201010000.do) announces partial closures in the Calligraphy and Painting and Sculpture and Crafts sections through 28 January 2027. Check the current notice if a particular gallery is the reason for going. Korea Tourism Organization’s [venue listing](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?menuSn=351&vcontsId=83891) also lists closures on New Year’s Day, Seollal and Chuseok, plus quarterly permanent-gallery closure days. Confirm the dated museum notice for your visit rather than copying an older itinerary.

This supports a simple winter rhythm: make the museum your main appointment, have a meal nearby that you have chosen separately, and leave any outdoor addition optional. The late opening on Wednesday or Saturday creates flexibility; it is not a recommendation to remain out until closing. A shorter visit that leaves everyone interested is often more useful than exhausting the group in the name of seeing everything.

## Choose the month as carefully as the city

A December holiday and a Lunar New Year holiday are not interchangeable. Climate descriptions describe seasons, while operating calendars describe individual days. A winter festival or skating rink may have its own opening period; neither a historical photo nor last year’s programme confirms the next edition.

Before committing to non-refundable bookings, make a small decision sheet:

| Write down | Why it changes the trip |
|---|---|
| The activity that makes the journey worthwhile | Distinguishes essential snow from an optional atmosphere |
| One indoor attraction and its exact opening date | Tests whether the weather alternative actually exists |
| The airport transfer plus the final hotel connection | Exposes stairs, outdoor walking and luggage decisions |
| One day with no fixed excursion | Gives disrupted or tiring plans somewhere to move |
| Cancellation terms for the snow activity | Makes the financial consequence of poor conditions explicit |

If the first row says skiing, this city comparison is only the starting point. Research lessons, resort access, equipment, medical considerations and relevant insurance separately. Snow on a pavement is not evidence of suitable conditions at a ski area, and urban walking equipment is not a substitute for sport-specific advice.

## Pack for the plan you actually bought

Separate urban walking from snow play, skiing or other outdoor activities. They may require different equipment and instruction. Before buying clothing, write down the activities and how long you expect to be outside. Ask the relevant operator what is included and what must be brought.

For children, check how spare clothing will travel with you. For older relatives or anyone with limited mobility, ask concrete access questions: distance outside, steps, seating and the availability of assistance. This article does not certify any of these cities or a named route as accessible for every traveller.

A spare afternoon is useful equipment too. Keep one part of the itinerary available for rest or a moved visit, and avoid building a holiday whose success depends on perfect conditions every day.

## Make the final decision before the first booking

Ask each traveller to finish this sentence: “I would still enjoy the trip if…” If the answer depends on snow falling outside the hotel window, Tokyo is a weak match for that expectation. If the answer depends on avoiding wintry surfaces, Sapporo requires a more careful conversation.

For Seoul, the same test applies: would the galleries, food and neighbourhoods still justify the journey without a snowfall? If not, revise the destination or activity plan before purchasing it.

Browse [Arahkaii's travel edit](https://www.arahkaii.com/travel/) for other planning approaches, then use the relevant city and transport operators for current conditions. The aim is not a winter holiday with no uncertainty. It is a trip whose ordinary days remain worth having when the weather changes the plan.
