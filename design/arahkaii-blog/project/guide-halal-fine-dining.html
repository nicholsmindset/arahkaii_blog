The 10 best halal fine-dining restaurants in Singapore (2026)

A year of unhurried tables — where the kitchen keeps faith with the calendar, the produce and the prayer.

By Farah Idris·2 June 2026·11 min read

01

Sembah

A modern-Malay tasting room where each course reads like a sentence in a longer essay about the archipelago. The cooking is precise without being fussy; the room is hushed and candle-low. Book the counter and let the kitchen lead.

Tanjong PagarModern Malay$$$$

✦
02

Marhaba

A Levantine kitchen working at the edge of fine dining, with charcoal at its heart and a pastry section that rivals any in the city. The mezze arrive slowly, deliberately, an argument for eating with your hands and your time.

BugisLevantine$$$

✦
03

Rumah Tujuh

Set in a restored shophouse, this is Peranakan cooking treated as heritage and as art. The rempah are pounded by hand each morning; the result is a depth of flavour that feels almost archival. A long lunch here is a small education.

KatongPeranakan$$$

✦
04

Noor

Contemporary Indian fine dining that resists nostalgia. The tasting menu moves between regions with confidence, and the vegetarian pairing is no afterthought. Soft light, softer service, and spice handled like a colourist handles pigment.

DempseyContemporary Indian$$$$

✦
05

Selat

A seafood-led room that treats the strait as a larder. Everything is line-caught and aged with patience; the menu changes with the tides. The wine list is alcohol-free by design and far more interesting for it.

Marina BayModern Seafood$$$$

✦
06

Tanah

Farm-to-table in the most literal sense, with a rooftop plot feeding a menu that reads the season closely. The cooking is quiet, vegetable-forward and deeply considered. A meal here feels like a conversation with the soil.

Tiong BahruModern Vegetable$$$

✦
07

Andalus

Moorish-Andalusian flavours filtered through a Singaporean lens, served around a planted courtyard. The breads come from a wood oven you can smell from the street. Order the long menu and stay until the lanterns come on.

Joo ChiatAndalusian$$$

✦
08

Mirah

A dessert-forward fine-dining concept that flips the meal on its head, building savoury into sweet with real rigour. The palm-sugar work is extraordinary. It should feel like a gimmick and instead feels inevitable.

OrchardModern Patisserie$$$

✦
09

Langit

A rooftop tasting room with a view it never leans on. The cooking is pan-Asian and restrained, the service unhurried to the point of grace. Come at dusk, when the room goes gold and the city quiets below.

Raffles PlacePan-Asian$$$$

✦
10

Sahaja

Eight seats, one nightly menu, and a chef who cooks as though no one is watching. The format is austere; the food is generous. It is the hardest reservation in the city, and worth every week of waiting.

Telok AyerChef's Counter$$$$

✦
